PYQ Behavior in Polity

Exam Pattern Insights

Polity is high-scoring and factual in Prelims. Historically, ~15–20 questions (15–17%) in each Prelims come from Polity. In 2014–2024 Prelims, ~88 Polity questions were asked.

  • High-yield areas: Parliament & Legislatures (~30 Qs) and Rights/DPSP/Duties (~29 Qs)
  • Question nature: Straightforward to moderate (90% accuracy possible); statement/trap formats dominate
  • Even seldom-directly-asked topics (e.g. Concept of Constitution) underpin many traps
  • Ranking criteria: (1) PYQ frequency, (2) elimination power, (3) conceptual dependency weight

⚡ Statement-Combination Traps

Most Polity MCQs use “which of the above statements is/are correct?” format. Article-number recall and boundary conditions dismantle these traps.

⚡ Article-Number Matching

UPSC frequently tests “which Article covers X?” or places wrong Article numbers in options. Systematic Article mapping is the top eliminator.

⚡ Institution vs Power Swaps

Traps swap institutions (President vs Governor, SC vs HC), powers (original vs appellate), or bodies (constitutional vs statutory).

⚡ Constitutional vs Statutory Distinction

“Is this body constitutional or statutory?” is a recurring elimination lever. Confusing NHRC (statutory) with UPSC (constitutional) is a classic trap.

#1 — Unit 6: Fundamental Rights (Part III, Arts 12–35)

HIGH YIELD • ~20+ PYQs
PYQ Footprint: ~20+ questions (2013–2024) Appearance: Direct factual • Statement traps • Elimination-heavy
Micro-TopicArticlesKey Points for Prelims
Definition of “State”Art. 12Who is bound by FR: includes Government, Parliament, state legislatures, local authorities, instrumentalities
Laws inconsistent with FRArt. 13Pre- and post-constitutional laws void if they violate Part III; constitutional basis for judicial review
Right to EqualityArts 14–18Equality before law; prohibition of discrimination on religion/caste/sex; abolition of titles & untouchability
Right to FreedomArts 19–226 freedoms (speech, assembly, association, movement, residence, profession); reasonable restrictions; preventive detention (Art. 22)
Right against ExploitationArts 23–24Prohibition of trafficking, forced labour, child labour
Freedom of ReligionArts 25–28Individual & communal rights; secularism; no tax for any religion
Cultural & Educational RightsArts 29–30Minority rights to conserve culture; manage educational institutions
Right to Constitutional RemediesArts 32–35Writs (habeas corpus, mandamus, etc.); Art. 32 as “Heart and Soul” of Constitution
Limitations on FRVariousNot absolute; subject to reasonable restrictions; 44th Amendment removed property as FR
FR vs DPSPDoctrineHarmony principle (Minerva Mills case); neither can override the other absolutely
Landmark casesDoctrineKesavananda (basic structure, FR immutability); Minerva Mills (harmonizing FR-DPSP)
Past Year Question Pattern
Tested directly (“Which right is NOT a FR?”) or via statements. Questions involve article identification, nature of rights, key cases, writ jurisdiction. FR knowledge also eliminates wrong options across unrelated Polity questions.
Why Placed First
FR are the foundation of rights discourse and judicial review. Mastery is crucial for many downstream topics (Art. 13, JR, Amendments). Both direct scoring and elimination-trap heavy. Provides base for understanding DPSP conflicts and basic-structure issues.
Prerequisites
None. Basic awareness of “State” (Art. 12) and Article 13 helpful.

#2 — Unit 7: Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV, Arts 36–51)

HIGH YIELD • ~10–15 PYQs
PYQ Footprint: ~10–15 questions Appearance: Conceptual • Article-mapping • Elimination-based
Micro-TopicArticlesKey Points for Prelims
Overview & NatureArts 36–51Non-justiciable guidelines for State; social & economic welfare; Art. 37 says “not enforceable by courts”
Classification of DPSPsVariousGandhian: Arts 40–48 • Socialistic: 39A, 41–43 • International: 51 • Environmental: 48A
Key Principles39(d), 40, 43, 48A, 45Equal pay, village panchayats, living wage, environment, education clause
DPSP vs FR relationshipDoctrineHarmonization (Minerva Mills); no absolute conflict
Enforcement & UseArt. 37Courts can interpret laws in light of DPSPs; used for policy evaluation
Past Year Question Pattern
“Which of the following is a DPSP?” — identification by article number or mapping ideals to articles. Also tested via interplay with FR. Strong elimination-based questions.
Why Placed Here
DPSPs form the ideological basis of the Constitution, complementing FR. Understanding DPSPs enhances elimination accuracy on questions contrasting rights and duties. They tie into basic structure (socialist pattern).
Prerequisites
Fundamental Rights (Unit 6) for contrast. General Article-number literacy (Arts 36–51).

#3 — Unit 8: Fundamental Duties (Art. 51A)

HIGH YIELD • ~5–10 PYQs
PYQ Footprint: ~5–10 questions Appearance: Statement-based • True/False identification • Elimination
Micro-TopicKey Points
Art. 51A list (11 duties)Upholding Constitution & sovereignty; promoting harmony; cherishing heritage; environmental protection; safeguarding public property; abjuring violence; educating children
NatureNon-justiciable (no enforcement through courts), similar to DPSPs; bind citizens (not the State)
OriginAdded by 42nd Amendment (1976), along with “Integrity” in Preamble
Common traps“Which is NOT a duty?” — some items confused with statutory obligations. Number of duties (11) frequently tested.
Why Placed Here
Part of the FR/DPSP/Duties triad tested together (~29 Qs total in the category). Though low standalone scoring, knowing Duties is crucial for elimination and concept clarity (“constitutional vs statutory obligations”).
Prerequisites
FR and DPSPs (to see them as counterparts; recall that Duties are not judicially enforceable).

#4 — Unit 10: Parliament (Union Legislature)

HIGHEST PYQ DENSITY • ~15–20 PYQs
PYQ Footprint: ~15–20 questions (single most-tested topic) Appearance: Direct factual • Statement-based • Procedure traps
Micro-TopicKey Points
Composition — Lok SabhaUp to 543 elected MPs + 2 Anglo-Indian nominees; term 5 years; directly elected by FPTP
Composition — Rajya Sabha245 seats (incl. 12 nominated); ⅓ retire every 2 years; elected by state assemblies via proportional representation
Legislative PowersJoint procedure for ordinary bills; Money Bills originate in LS, RS can only recommend within 14 days; Constitutional Amendment bills (Art. 368)
Parliamentary ProceduresSessions, Question Hour, Zero Hour, no-confidence motions, joint sittings; President’s address (Art. 87)
PrivilegesFreedom from arrest in civil cases; free speech in House
Anti-Defection (10th Schedule)Disqualification grounds for leaving party or defying whip; merger exception (91st Amendment abolished ⅓ split)
Past Year Question Pattern
Tested via composition facts (LS strength: 543, RS term), legislative procedure (money bills, where revenue bills originate), Speaker’s powers. Also statement-based: “Which is/are correct about Parliament?”
Why Placed Here
As the core of legislature, understanding Parliament is directly scoring (clear factual questions) and crucial for elimination (invalid bills, quorums). Builds on Union Executive and precedes State Legislature by analogy.
Prerequisites
Union Executive (President’s role in Parliament). Basic Constitution concept. Mostly self-contained once house basics are known.

#6 — Unit 15: Supreme Court (Part V, Arts 124–147)

HIGH YIELD • ~5–8 PYQs
PYQ Footprint: ~5–8 questions Appearance: Direct factual • Jurisdiction traps • Composition questions
Micro-TopicKey Points
CompositionCJI + other judges (currently 34); appointed by President on Collegium recommendation (CJI + 4 seniormost judges); removal by Parliament impeachment; retire at 65
Original JurisdictionDisputes involving states or Centre (Art. 131); election disputes
Appellate JurisdictionCivil & criminal appeals; constitutional cases need 5-judge bench
Writ Jurisdiction (Art. 32)Enforce FRs: habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari, quo warranto
Advisory Jurisdiction (Art. 143)President may seek SC opinion on legal matters
Transfer of Cases (Art. 139A)SC can withdraw cases from HC if common question
Why Placed Here
Supreme Court is heavily tested and feeds into judicial review & constitutional amendment discussions. Strong direct scoring (facts about jurisdiction, bench size) and elimination power (trap statements on advisory vs appellate, quorums).
Prerequisites
None (general civics sufficient). Part V context from Unit 2 helps.

#7 — Unit 20: Judicial Review (Arts 13, 32, 226)

HIGH YIELD • CONCEPTUAL CORE
PYQ Footprint: ~5–7 questions Appearance: Conceptual • Statement-based • High elimination value
Micro-TopicKey Points
Art. 13 — Constitutional basis“All laws inconsistent with Part III shall be void” — foundation for judicial review in India
Writ PowersSC (Art. 32) and HCs (Art. 226) issue writs to enforce FR and legal rights; Art. 32 is itself a Fundamental Right
ScopeAny statute affecting FR can be struck down (void ab initio); HCs also review executive actions (Art. 226)
Kesavananda Bharati (1973)JR is a “fundamental characteristic” of the Constitution; Parliament cannot alter basic structure
L. Chandra Kumar (1997)JR is integral to basic structure; writ jurisdiction extends over tribunals
Why Placed Here
JR underlies almost all parts of the Constitution. It is a foundational concept that unlocks Amendment (Unit 30) and Basic Structure (Unit 21). High elimination value — negates wrong statements about law validity, FR suspension, and amendment limits.
Prerequisites
FR (Unit 6) to know which rights are enforced. Supreme Court (Unit 15) for which court exercises JR.

#15 — Unit 21: Basic Structure Doctrine

MEDIUM YIELD • ~3–5 PYQs
Micro-TopicKey Points
DefinitionNo explicit Article; judicially invented doctrine (Kesavananda 1973) that certain core features cannot be amended
Basic FeaturesPreamble ideals, judicial review, federalism, sovereignty, dignity, parliamentary system (no exhaustive list)
Key CasesKesavananda Bharati (1973): Parliament’s power unlimited under Art. 368 but cannot alter basic structure. Minerva Mills, Indira Gandhi cases reaffirm.
ApplicationAny amendment abrogating core features is void (e.g. NJAC struck down)
Why Placed Here
Bridge between judicial review and amendment process. Without it, one could wrongly assume Parliament’s power is absolute. Improves elimination on any amendment or FR-conflict question.
Prerequisites
Judicial Review (Unit 20). FR (Unit 6) — content that’s part of basic structure.
