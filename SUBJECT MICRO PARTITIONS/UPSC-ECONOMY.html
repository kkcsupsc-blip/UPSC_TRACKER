What “Economy” Means in Prelims (PYQ-Aligned)

UPSC does not restrict Economy to textbook macroeconomics. It repeatedly tests:

  • Institutional design — who does what, statutory vs constitutional anchoring
  • Instrument–purpose matching — what tool does what and under what conditions
  • Definitions with boundary conditions — near-synonym traps, inclusion/exclusion
  • Current-affairs-triggered frameworks — new RBI tool, index base revision, new IMF facility, etc.

The exam pattern structurally supports statement-combination traps and elimination-style scoring. The most valuable topics are those that (i) recur, (ii) remove options across unrelated questions, and (iii) reduce ambiguity in statement verification.

PYQ Behavior: Dominant Constructs, Statement Traps & Elimination Levers

Instrument → Purpose → Collateral → Corridor

Highest-frequency pattern, especially in monetary policy and liquidity management. Typical traps swap: “inject vs absorb liquidity,” “collateralised vs uncollateralised,” “floor vs ceiling,” “repo vs reverse repo,” “MSF vs Bank Rate,” “policy rate vs operating target.”

Institution → Composition → Mandate → Anchoring

High-return pattern for GST Council, MPC, CBDT/CBIC, etc. Composition anchored in specific articles/statutes. Traps swap membership/functions.

Definition Boundary Traps

Dominant in national income, inflation, external sector, and fiscal concepts. Examples: GDP production/expenditure/income approaches, CPI as inflation targeting index, REER/NEER as competitiveness indicators.

Current Affairs Framed as Concepts

Standard UPSC move: the question is not “news recall” but “what does this facility/tool/index do?” Example: “lender of last resort” (Prelims 2021) tested as concept, not as current affairs.

Exhaustive Unit-by-Unit Micro-Topic Map (PYQ Order)

#1 — Unit 1: Introduction to Economics

HIGH YIELD (Prerequisite-Heavy)
PYQ Footprint: ~10–20 questions Appearance: Conceptual • Statement-combination • Elimination-heavy
Topic BlockYieldPYQ Est.TypeKey Micro-Topics
Basics of economic reasoning & vocabulary High ~6–10 Conceptual + Statement-combination Scarcity; choice; opportunity cost; PPF shifts vs movements; trade-offs & “cost” in economic sense
Microeconomics core constructs Medium ~3–6 Statement-combination Demand vs quantity demanded; elasticity (price, income); substitutes vs complements; normal vs inferior vs Giffen; consumer/producer surplus
Market structures & pricing power Low–Med ~1–3 Conceptual Perfect competition / monopoly / monopolistic / oligopoly (features for MCQ distinctions only)
Macroeconomics core constructs High ~6–10 Conceptual + Elimination-heavy AD vs AS as idea; business cycle phases; recession/stagnation; output gap; nominal vs real; stocks vs flows; circular flow
Goods vs services; factor vs product markets Medium ~1–3 Statement-based eliminators Factor income vs transfer; productive vs unproductive in national accounts sense
Externalities & public goods Medium ~2–4 Statement-combination Externality vs spillover; positive/negative; non-rival, non-excludable; common pool resources; free-rider
Market failure & govt intervention vocabulary Medium ~2–4 Conceptual Price ceiling/floor effects; rationing; deadweight loss concept; subsidies & incidence intuition
Why This Unit is First
This unit is the conceptual grammar of Economy MCQs: most statement traps in later units reuse these words with altered boundary conditions (“real vs nominal,” “stock vs flow,” “transfer vs factor income,” “public good vs merit good”). Treating it as optional leads to systematic elimination errors across Units 2, 5, 7, and 14.
Prerequisites
None.

#2 — Unit 2: National Income Accounting

HIGH YIELD
PYQ Footprint: ~15–30 questions Appearance: Statement-combination + conceptual definitions; numeracy-light but definition-heavy
Topic BlockYieldPYQ Est.TypeKey Micro-Topics
GDP, GVA, Value Added — definitions & relationships High ~8–14 Statement-combination + Elimination-heavy Value added = output − intermediate consumption; GDP (production) = Σ value added + taxes net of subsidies on products; GVA vs GDP distinction
Three approaches to GDP High ~6–10 Statement-combination Production approach vs expenditure (PFCE, GFCE, GFCF, change in stocks, net exports) vs income approach (COE, GOS, mixed income); “discrepancy” concept
Nominal vs Real; deflators High ~6–10 Conceptual + Elimination-heavy Deflator logic (current/constant); index logic; base year meaning; chain linking concept
GDP vs GNP; domestic vs national Medium ~2–5 Statement-combination “Resident” concept; factor income from abroad; NFIA; NNP/NDP distinctions
Savings, investment, capital formation Medium ~2–5 Conceptual GFCF vs gross capital formation; depreciation/CFC; capital-output intuition
Sectoral composition & structural change Medium ~2–4 Conceptual Primary/secondary/tertiary shares; what “services” covers; measurement sensitivity
Base-year revision (CA trigger) Medium ~1–3 Direct factual + Statement traps What revision changes (weights, data sources, methods); why measured growth rates can shift
Why This Placement
National income accounting is placed before money/fiscal/inflation because Prelims repeatedly tests definitions that sit inside other questions. If GDP/GVA/current/constant/deflator boundaries are unclear, elimination fails even when current affairs are known.

#3 — Unit 3: Money, Banking & Monetary Policy

TOP SCORER + TOP ELIMINATOR
PYQ Footprint: ~45–65 questions Appearance: Statement-combination + Elimination-heavy
Topic BlockYieldPYQ Est.TypeKey Micro-Topics
Monetary policy framework & decision structure High ~8–12 Statement-combination MPC decides policy repo rate for inflation target; meeting frequency/quorum; voting/casting vote logic; WACR as operating target aligned to repo via liquidity management
Policy rates & liquidity corridor instruments High ~12–18 Elimination-heavy Repo: liquidity injected against govt securities collateral • Reverse Repo: absorb liquidity against eligible securities • SDF: uncollateralised deposits, 25 bps below repo, floor of corridor • MSF: penal overnight borrowing dipping into SLR, 25 bps above repo • LAF corridor: MSF ceiling, SDF floor, repo in middle • CRR/SLR: definitional boundaries, gazette-notified
Bank Rate & its linkage High ~3–6 Statement-combination Published under Section 49 of RBI Act; aligned with MSF; penal rate for reserve shortfalls
Money supply & liquidity aggregates High ~6–10 Conceptual + Elimination Monetary base / high powered money; broad money; credit creation; money multiplier; NDTL role; liquidity surplus/deficit logic
Banking system structure & regulated entities High ~8–12 Statement-combination Scheduled vs non-scheduled; commercial vs cooperative vs RRBs; SFB/payment bank; NBFC vs bank; deposit-taking vs non-deposit NBFC
Bank balance sheet & core risks Medium ~3–6 Statement-combination NPAs; provisioning; CRAR; risk-weighted assets; liquidity risk vs solvency risk
“Lender of last resort” & emergency liquidity Medium ~1–3 Direct + Elimination LoLR = liquidity support to banks in temporary crisis; trap is “lending to government deficits” (Prelims 2021)
Payments systems & monetary transmission Medium ~2–5 Conceptual (CA-framed) Transmission: policy rate → money market → bank lending; why liquidity management matters for WACR
Why This is the Top Scoring Unit
UPSC repeatedly uses RBI instruments as statement-trap payloads, and the same vocabulary appears as distractors in questions not “about monetary policy.” RBI’s formal, published definitions make elimination deterministic when learned properly.
Prerequisites
Unit 1 (macro vocabulary), Unit 2 (real vs nominal; deflator logic), plus basic “stocks vs flows.”
