Help & Scheduling Logic

Understand how the Option C dynamic scheduling algorithm works under the hood

🎯 Overview
This tracker uses a 12-stage spaced repetition pipeline combined with Option C dynamic scheduling to maximise your daily study budget.

The Problem (Old System): Each of the 12 revision stages was assigned a separate calendar day. If a stage only took 1h but your daily budget was 6h, the remaining 5h were wasted. Over 12 stages, this meant ~11 days of sub-optimal utilisation per topic.

The Solution (Option C): Stages that don't need neurological spacing between them are packed together on the same day, forming compact "blocks". The timeline estimator then uses budget-aware dynamic packing to slide stages into the first day that has room — no wasted hours.

⚡ Two-Phase Dynamic Scheduling
Instead of spreading 12 stages across 12 separate days, the scheduler uses a two-phase architecture that separates rigid spaced-repetition stages from flexible practice clusters:
Phase 1 — Rigid Stages (Spaced Repetition + Deep Work)
These get placed onto the calendar first, respecting minimum neurological gaps:
StageHoursEarliest DateRule
R11.5 hR1 interval (default 1d)First recall — needs at least one sleep cycle
R31 hR3 interval (default 5d), min 1d after R1Active recall at optimal forgetting point
MN ⭐1 h1 day after R3Standalone — no other stages of this topic share its day
R71.5 hR7 interval (default 14d), min 5d after MN, Sunday onlyWeekend consolidation after forgetting curve dip
MVA1 h7 days after R7Fresh perspective for mains depth
MAINS1.5 h1 day after MVAAnswer writing with fresh value addition
R301 hR30 interval (default 35d), min 14d after MAINSFinal long-term consolidation
Phase 2 — Flexible Clusters (Practice & Analysis)
After all rigid stages are placed, these practice clusters fill remaining budget gaps as atomic units:
ClusterStagesHoursPlaced FromRule
A PYQ + ERA 1 h R1 day onward Practice + error analysis — packs onto R1 day if budget allows
B CA + MCQ + MCQA 2 h Day after MN Applied practice — mapping, testing, analysis. Never on MN day.
Why Two-Phase? With multiple topics, a topic's flexible PYQ or MCQ practice could steal budget from another topic's critical R3 recall. The global two-phase design ensures all rigid stages across all topics are placed before any flexible cluster — spaced repetition is sacred.
Result: 12 stages in only ~8 calendar dates per topic (R1+PYQ+ERA share Day 1, CA+MCQ+MCQA share one day). MN gets its own dedicated deep-focus slot.

📅 Budget-Aware Dynamic Packing (Estimator)
The Timeline Estimator goes one step further with budget-aware packing:
  1. For each revision stage, it computes the earliest possible date (respecting minimum gaps).
  2. It checks if the day's budget has enough room for the stage's hours (after existing revisions + learning).
  3. If the day is full, the stage slides to the next available day with room.
  4. When a stage is placed, its hours are committed to that day's budget, affecting all subsequent scheduling.

This means the estimator accounts for:
  • Global two-phase priority: All topics' rigid stages placed before any flexible clusters
  • Multiple topics competing for the same day's budget
  • Weekday (6h) vs Weekend (12h) capacity differences
  • Atomic flexible clusters: PYQ+ERA and CA+MCQ+MCQA are placed as whole units — no splitting
  • MN isolation: Note-making days never share with other stages of the same topic
  • Learning hours for active topics reducing available budget
⏰ Configurable Intervals
Four key intervals can be customised in Settings → Revision Intervals:
IntervalDefaultControls
R11 dayDays from completion to first recall (Phase 1)
R35 daysDays from completion to active recall (Phase 1, min 1d after R1)
R714 daysDays from completion to consolidation (Phase 1, Sunday, min 5d after MN)
R3035 daysDays from completion to final revision (Phase 1, min 14d after MAINS)
These are anchor points. The actual date is max(anchor, previous_stage + min_gap), so reducing an interval never creates an impossible schedule.
🚀 Timeline Estimator — How It Simulates
The estimator on the Timeline page runs a day-by-day simulation up to 400 days:
  1. Step 1 — Real data: Already completed/pipeline-complete topics use their actual dates.
  2. Step 2 — Build budget map: All pending revisions + cumulative sessions are loaded into a daily-hours map.
  3. Step 3 — Queue topics: Not-started topics are sorted by ROI priority, then addition order.
  4. Step 4 — Simulate each day:
    • Compute revision load from the budget map
    • Available = daily budget − revision load
    • Spend available hours on active learning topics
    • When a topic finishes learning → dynamically schedule its 12 revision stages (budget-aware)
    • Start new topics from queue if parallel slots are free

🔄 Cumulative Revision System
After a topic reaches Pipeline Complete status (all stages done), it enters the Cumulative Revision system:
  • Sectional Batches: Every 5–10 pipeline-complete topics form a cross-subject batch. 3 revision rounds at increasing intervals (0, 21, 56 days).
  • Subject Revisions: When all high-ROI topics in a subject are pipeline-complete, 4 rounds of full-subject revision (14, 42, 98, 168 days).
  • Both are scheduled on Saturdays for 2-day weekend blocks.
